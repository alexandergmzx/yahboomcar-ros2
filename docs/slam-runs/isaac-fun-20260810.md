## Updated audit verdict — 2026-08-10

**Turning speed is the primary failure boundary.** Alex's manual-driving
observations are that straight motion maps normally, ordinary/faster turns
break the map, and slower driving is substantially more stable. The bags agree
with the directional mechanism: during straight motion the median truth/odom
speed ratios are 1.049 and 1.050 (about 5% scale error), while turn yaw is
over-reported by 2.82× and 2.95×. That angular prior error makes slam_toolbox
re-lay otherwise crisp walls at the wrong heading after each turn. Lower speed
plausibly reduces slip and per-scan angular mismatch, but this session did not
record a speed-controlled A/B. Replacing the odometry prior with truth
changes both failed maps into passing 4.16/4.14 m rooms. The replay replaces the
full SE(2) prior, so a yaw-only arm remains the strict isolation experiment, but
the turn-only symptom and order-of-magnitude difference between linear and
angular error make encoder yaw the strongly supported dominant component.

**Close-box failure is a separate, open boundary.** Alex's follow-up manual
test found that the slow map can still destabilize when the car gets too close
to a fun-mode box. That observation does not separate (a) wheel/chassis contact
and extra slip, (b) the 0.02 kg box moving through a map that assumes a static
world, or (c) residual near-field lidar dropout. The surviving 12:02–12:06
rolling logs show 2 laser-odometry degeneracies out of 2,885 scans, one raw scan
rejected by the relay, and 89 SLAM queue-full drops. They record neither command
speed nor box distance and have no bag or saved map, so those events cannot be
assigned to the close-box incident.

**Correction:** the original “27% of scans ≥0.2 s stale” result below was an
instrument artifact. The scratch probe did not reject static motion even though
time-offset fitting is unobservable when the robot is parked. Applying the
later mandatory guard excludes 47/124 run-1 (46 static + one history-boundary)
and 43/121 run-2 samples (42 static + one boundary); the
guarded results are median −0.04 s in both runs, with only 1/77 (1.3%) and 2/78
(2.6%) moving samples at ≥0.2 s and no −0.6 s boundary hits. The render-pacing
controller still demonstrably diverges, but this session does not demonstrate
material content lag or map damage from it. The earlier 27% and 14° claims are
retracted everywhere this report states its conclusions.
